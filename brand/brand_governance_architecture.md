## 1. Purpose

This document defines how the NarrativeOS brand is governed: who owns it, how
decisions are made, what standards every asset must meet, and how compliance is
maintained as the platform and its generated assets scale.

The goal is simple: every asset that leaves NarrativeOS - a battle card, an
objection guide, a pitch deck - should feel like it came from one company with
one voice, regardless of who generated it or which module produced it.

## 2. Governance Model

The brand is governed in four tiers. Each tier has a clear owner and a clear
decision scope.

Tier 1 - Brand Steward (Owner). Final authority on brand identity, naming, the
master narrative, and any change to this document. Single accountable owner.

Tier 2 - Brand Council. Cross-functional reviewers (product marketing, design,
sales enablement). Approves new asset templates and major messaging shifts.
Meets on a fixed cadence.

Tier 3 - Module Owners. Each NarrativeOS module (Product Understanding,
Positioning, Story Architecture, Battle Cards, and so on) has an owner who keeps
that module's output aligned with the standards below.

Tier 4 - Contributors. Anyone generating assets. Operates inside approved
templates and cannot alter brand-level rules.

## 3. Decision Rights (RACI Summary)

| Decision | Steward | Council | Module Owner | Contributor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Logo / wordmark change | A | C | I | I |
| Master narrative change | A | C | C | I |
| New asset template | C | A | R | I |
| Tone / voice guidelines | A | C | C | I |
| Publishing a single asset | I | I | A | R |

A = Accountable, R = Responsible, C = Consulted, I = Informed.

## 4. Brand Standards Every Asset Must Meet

Naming. Always "NarrativeOS" (one word, capital N, capital OS). Never "Narrative
OS" or "narrativeOS". Tagline reference: "The operating system for product
storytelling." Attribution line where used: "Powered by StoryGTM."

Voice. Confident, clear, and useful. Short sentences. Concrete claims over
adjectives. No hype words that cannot be backed by evidence.

Narrative spine. Every asset traces back to one master narrative (see the Master
Brand deck). Individual assets may zoom into a slice of it but must not
contradict it.

Evidence. Any competitive or performance claim needs a source or a clearly
labelled assumption. "Be honest" sections in battle cards are mandatory, not
optional.

## 5. Asset Lifecycle

Draft -> Review -> Approved -> Published -> Retired.

Draft: generated by a module or contributor.
Review: checked against Section 4 by the relevant Module Owner.
Approved: cleared for external use; recorded in the Asset Library.
Published: live in a deck, page, or enablement tool.
Retired: superseded or out of date; kept for audit, removed from active reuse.

## 6. Asset Library Governance

The in-product Asset Library is the system of record for approved assets. Rules:
every saved asset carries a type, a title, and a created date; retired assets are
flagged rather than silently deleted; and the library is the single place teams
pull from, so no one recreates messaging from scratch.

## 7. Compliance and Review Cadence

Quarterly brand audit of a sample of live assets against Section 4. Any asset
that fails is routed back to Review. Repeated failures in one module trigger a
template revision by the Module Owner and Council.

## 8. Change Control

Changes to this document are proposed to the Council, decided by the Steward, and
versioned. Every change records what changed, why, and the effective date.
